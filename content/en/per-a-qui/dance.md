Dance is one of the performing arts disciplines where digital visibility has the least tradition. But festival programmers and venues now search online first. When they receive a submission, the first thing they do is look up the company's name in a browser. What they find in the next thirty seconds determines whether they keep reading the dossier.

A contemporary dance piece can be extraordinary and never reach any programme because the professional materials accompanying it are insufficient, outdated or impossible to find.

## Who is looking for you and where

Every opportunity comes with someone who has to make a quick decision about whether your proposal fits. These are the profiles that search for you:

## The specific tools for dance

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">01</span>
<strong>Company EPK.</strong> A dance EPK is different from a musician's EPK: it includes a complete technical rider (stage dimensions, rigging requirements, set-up and strike times, personnel needed), an artistic dossier, a synopsis in two formats (short and long), a team profile and the full rider. All accessible from a single link. Read the guide: <a href="/en/resources/epk-dance-circus/">EPK for dance and circus companies</a>.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">02</span>
<strong>High-resolution stage photos.</strong> The key distinction for dance: they must be action shots, not posed photos. A posed shot of performers on an empty stage communicates nothing about the piece. What a programmer needs is to see the movement, the lighting, the relationship between bodies and space. Ressona works with Pocallum for stage photography during rehearsals and premieres. Read: <a href="/en/resources/press-photos/">press photos for artists</a>.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">03</span>
<strong>Video of the specific piece.</strong> Not a company showreel: the video of the piece being proposed. A programmer needs to see what the work looks like from start to finish (or the best fifteen minutes), with proper sound and framing that allows them to read the stage space. A well-shot phone video is better than a professional video from seven years ago of a different piece.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">04</span>
<strong>Company website with downloadable technical rider.</strong> The website is the permanent centre of a company's digital identity. It must include a downloadable PDF technical rider, a stage photo gallery, videos, touring history, team bios and direct contact. Ressona works with LinuxBCN to build performing arts company websites optimised for search engines and easy to update.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">05</span>
<strong>Presence on European circuits.</strong> Circostrada, Aerowaves, On the Move: directories and networks of European programmers working specifically with contemporary performing arts. Appearing in them guarantees nothing, but not appearing systematically eliminates opportunities. Presence is the first step towards international distribution circuits.
</div>

</div>

<div class="resource-callout">
<h3>The first filter is visual</h3>
<p>A dance festival programmer receives hundreds of submissions. The first filter is visual: if you don't have quality stage photos, your dossier won't make it past the first click. It's not a question of aesthetics — it's a question of professional credibility.</p>
</div>
