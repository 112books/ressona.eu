When your profile photo is different across all your platforms, your website clashes with your poster design and your artist name is spelled three different ways, you're sending an unintentional message: a lack of professionalism, or at least a lack of attention to detail.

Visual identity isn't an aesthetic whim. It's the first layer of your artistic brand — what people see **before** they hear your music, see your show or read your bio.

## What visual identity is

Visual identity is the set of graphic elements that represent your work consistently: logo (or signature), colour palette, typography, photographic style and general visual tone. When all these elements work together, they create **recognition** — people know it's you in a second.

You don't need the most sophisticated logo in the world. You need **coherence**.

## The basic elements

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">01</span>

**Your name as a visual signature.** Many artists don't need an elaborate logo — they just need their name written in a consistent typeface. What you can't do is use random fonts every time. Choose one typeface (or two at most, one for headings and one for body text) and use them always.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">02</span>

**A palette of 3–4 colours.** A primary colour (the one that appears first), a supporting one, a contrasting one and a background. Less is more. If you don't know where to start, analyse your visual references: artists you admire, album covers you like, aesthetic of the spaces where you want to perform.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">03</span>

**A consistent profile photo.** Not just a good photo — the same photo (or the same style) across all platforms: Spotify, Instagram, Facebook, website, YouTube. When someone searches for you across multiple places at once, consistency builds trust.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">04</span>

**A visual style for your content.** If you post on social media, your posts should have a connecting thread: same palette, same title typography, same composition approach. They don't need to be identical — they need to look like they come from the same hand.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">05</span>

**A minimal brand guide.** Even if it's just a two-page document with your colours (in hex and CMYK), your fonts (with name and weight), and your official profile photo. When a festival asks for materials for their programme, you know exactly what to send.
</div>

</div>

## When a professional designer is worth it

You can start with tools like Canva or Adobe Express to create consistent content without being a designer. But there are moments where investing in a professional is worthwhile:

- **Launching a debut album or important show**: the materials need to be impeccable
- **Rebranding**: if you've changed artistic style or name and need to renew your image
- **Professional presence at markets and festivals**: where first impressions literally come in a printed folder
- **Website**: an amateur design undermines everything else you build around it

Professional design is an investment, not an expense — especially when you factor in the time you'll waste trying to do it yourself without experience.

## Coherence is the destination, not the starting point

You don't need to have everything perfect from day one. What you need is to:

1. Decide some basic elements (name spelled one way, a couple of colours, one typeface)
2. Apply them consistently from today onwards
3. Refine as your project grows

Visual identity is a living system — it will evolve with you. What kills credibility isn't having a simple design: it's having an incoherent one.
