Ten years ago, at the end of a concert you could sell CDs at the merch table. People bought them to show support, to take something home, to own a physical piece of the project. Many never even played them — but they bought them because they wanted the artist to keep going.

Today most people don't have a CD player. But the will to support is exactly the same. What's changed is the format.

Here are the alternatives that actually work today.

## The real problem

It's not that people don't want to pay. It's that they don't know how to do it comfortably. If you don't offer them a clear, immediate way to support you at the end of the concert, they leave and the impulse is gone. The window of opportunity is five minutes.

The solution is to have a clear, fast action ready that the fan can complete with their phone while they're still in the room.

## QR code: the simplest thing that works

A QR code printed on a poster, card or even a t-shirt takes the fan directly to:

- Your Bandcamp page to buy the album
- A page on your website with a free or paid download button
- Your Ko-fi or bank transfer for a direct donation
- Your Spotify or Apple Music playlist to follow your project

**How to do it well:**
- The QR must be large (minimum 5×5 cm for a card, much bigger for a venue poster)
- Test it in low light conditions — many QR codes fail in the dark of a concert venue
- Put clear text below the QR: "Listen & buy" / "Free download" / "Support the project"
- Use a URL shortener (bit.ly, or your own redirect) so you can change the destination without reprinting

**Recommended format:** A6 postcards (10×15 cm) — cheap to print, easy to hand out, people keep them.

## NFC: tap your phone and done

NFC (Near Field Communication) cards work like a QR but without needing a camera: the fan holds their phone near the card and the URL you've programmed opens automatically.

**Who it works for:** any iPhone from the 7 (iOS 14+) and any modern Android. Most current phones support it natively.

**How to program them:** with an app like NFC Tools (Android/iOS) you can write a URL onto an NFC card in under 30 seconds. Blank NFC cards cost between €0.30 and €1 each.

**Use cases:**
- An NFC card on the merch table: the fan taps it and goes straight to Bandcamp
- An NFC sticker on the back of a microphone clip: anyone who walks past can "tap" the mic
- An NFC embedded in a designed postcard: physical object + digital action

NFC is the best option for contexts where light is very low (dark concert venues) or where people don't want to open their camera.

## Download cards with unique codes

Download cards work like gift codes: the fan receives a physical card with a code they exchange for a music download on your website.

**How to implement it:**
- **Bandcamp**: has a built-in download code system. Generate as many codes as you want, print them on cards, and each code can be redeemed once.
- **Own website**: you can implement a simple system (a spreadsheet of codes + a redemption page on your site) or use tools like Downloadable (a WooCommerce plugin).

**Key advantage:** the physical card is an object. People keep it, touch it, see it in their wallet. It has symbolic value that a QR doesn't. You can design them as artwork in themselves.

## USB drives: small, cheap and surprising

USB drives have become so affordable they can be used as a distribution format. A 2GB USB (enough for an album in FLAC + PDF booklet + photos) costs under €1 each in runs of 100.

**What you can include:**
- Music in FLAC and MP3
- A PDF booklet (lyrics, texts, credits, photos)
- High-resolution photographs
- Videos (music videos, live recordings)
- A text note from the artist

**Recommended format:** card USBs — as thin as a credit card, fit in a wallet, and can be printed directly with your design. Search "card USB printing" — there are specialist services.

The USB is the option closest to the CD experience: a physical object containing the music. But unlike CDs, everyone has a USB port.

## Bank transfer + manual response

The most basic and effective option for small concerts: a visible sign with your bank transfer details or payment QR, and you manually send the download link to whoever pays you.

It doesn't scale well for high volume, but for concerts of 50–100 people it works perfectly and requires no prior infrastructure.

**Upgrade:** set up an automatic response via Telegram bot that sends the link automatically when it receives a payment with a specific reference ("album", "thanks", etc.). Requires a little technical setup but eliminates manual work.

## Merch as a distribution vector

Physical merch (t-shirts, bags, posters, vinyl) can integrate digital distribution:

- **QR on the t-shirt**: the buyer carries a QR leading to your music. Everyone who sees the t-shirt can scan it.
- **QR on packaging**: any merch bag or wrapper can carry a QR on the back.
- **NFC on vinyl**: if you press vinyl, an NFC sticker inside the sleeve takes the fan directly to the digital version or your website.

The t-shirt the fan wears on the street is advertising. If it carries a working QR, it's ongoing distribution.

## Community channels: WhatsApp and Telegram

At the end of the concert, invite the audience to join a channel:

- **Telegram channel** or **WhatsApp Broadcast**: where you'll announce new releases, dates and exclusive content. The fan joins now, while the emotion of the concert is fresh, and stays connected to the project.

Combined with a QR on the venue poster, it's one of the most effective ways to convert concert-goers into active followers.

## The message that works

Having the tool isn't enough. You have to communicate it. Some phrases that work from the stage:

> "If you enjoyed the music and want to take it home, we have a QR down here. You can download the album free or pay whatever you like."

> "At the merch table we have cards with a code to download the record. It really helps if you share it."

> "If you want to follow the project, join our channel — the QR code is on the poster at the door."

People want to support. You just need to tell them how.

## Summary by concert type

| Context | Best option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Large venue (200+ people) | QR on visible poster + cards at merch | Scales well, low cost |
| Small venue (up to 100) | NFC cards + bank transfer/Ko-fi | Tactile, immediate experience |
| Street concert | Large QR + visible payment info | No infrastructure needed, fast |
| Bar or small space | NFC at the bar + QR | Discreet, always visible |
| Festival (shared merch) | Download cards + USB | Physical object people keep |
