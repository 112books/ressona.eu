There is a route that connects Europe's jazz capitals without unnecessary detours. Ten cities, ten scenes, two possible weeks. From the clubs on Rue des Lombards in Paris to the canals of Amsterdam, the lakeside stage at Montreux and the bay of La Concha in San Sebastián.

This is an opinionated route, not an exhaustive directory. For a complete listing of clubs and venues by country, see [Travelling with Jazz Across Europe](/en/resources/travelling-with-jazz-europe/).

---

## The route: 10 cities, 10 scenes

### 1. Paris — The natural starting point

Paris has the highest density of jazz clubs in Europe. The heart is **Rue des Lombards**, in the Marais: within a hundred metres you'll find Duc des Lombards, Sunset/Sunside and Baiser Salé — three clubs with nightly programming and sessions until 3am. The **Jazz sur Seine** festival (October) transforms the whole city for fifteen days, with 25 clubs and hundreds of concerts, many free.

**Club:** [Duc des Lombards](https://www.ducdeslombards.com/) · **Festival:** [Jazz sur Seine](https://www.parisjazzclub.net/) (October)

---

### 2. London — High energy, non-stop programming

London runs like a jazz machine: Ronnie Scott's, open since 1959 in Soho, programmes concerts seven nights a week with international artists and extended residencies. The **EFG London Jazz Festival** (November) stretches from major venues like the Barbican to south London pubs.

**Club:** [Ronnie Scott's](https://www.ronniescotts.co.uk/) · **Festival:** [EFG London Jazz Festival](https://www.efglondonjazzfestival.org.uk/) (November)

---

### 3. Copenhagen — The Scandinavian jazz capital

The **Copenhagen Jazz Festival** (July, 10 days) turns the city into the world's largest open-air jazz festival: 1,500 concerts, many free in parks, squares and along the canals. **Jazzhus Montmartre**, founded in 1959, is the most legendary club in Scandinavian jazz history — Miles Davis, Bill Evans and Dexter Gordon all played here in its golden years, and the current programme maintains the standard.

**Club:** [Jazzhus Montmartre](https://jazzhusmontmartre.dk/) · **Festival:** [Copenhagen Jazz Festival](https://jazz.dk/en/) (July)

---

### 4. Amsterdam — Jazz inside a neighbourhood festival

Amsterdam combines its club scene with **Red Light Jazz** (June), which turns the streets of the old centre into an open jazz circuit for three days. **Bimhuis**, since 1974 by the harbour, is Europe's reference for improvisation and avant-garde jazz: a venue that built its reputation by programming what nobody else dared.

**Club:** [Bimhuis](https://www.bimhuis.com/) · **Festival:** [Red Light Jazz](https://redlightjazz.com/) (June)

---

### 5. Berlin — The European capital of avant-garde jazz

**Jazzfest Berlin**, founded in 1964, is one of Europe's most curatorially ambitious jazz festivals: it programmes what's interesting, not what sells. **Quasimodo**, since 1975, is where the Berlin scene meets night after night. Berlin is the city for those who want to understand post-war European jazz, free improvisation and the intersection of jazz and electronic music.

**Club:** [Quasimodo](https://www.quasimodo.de/) · **Festival:** [Jazzfest Berlin](https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/en/jazzfest-berlin) (November)

---

### 6. Vienna — The Central European jazz tradition

Vienna carries the reputation of a classical music capital, but it has a solid and international jazz scene. **Porgy & Bess**, open since 1993, is the centre of gravity of Viennese jazz: a venue respected across Europe, with programming that ranges from mainstream to the most challenging improvisation. One of the best examples of a long-running independent jazz club.

**Club:** [Porgy & Bess](https://www.porgy.at/) · **Festival:** Jazz Fest Wien (July)

---

### 7. Montreux — The living legend on Lake Geneva

The **Montreux Jazz Festival** (July, two weeks) is the jazz festival with the most legendary aura in Europe. Miles Davis, Ray Charles, David Bowie, Prince — the Casino Montreux stage has seen everything. Founded by Claude Nobs in 1967, the festival has grown to fill the entire lakefront with free outdoor stages and ticketed concerts. In 2026: 3–18 July.

**Festival:** [Montreux Jazz Festival](https://www.montreuxjazzfestival.com/en/) (3–18 July 2026)

---

### 8. Barcelona — The Mediterranean node

Barcelona enters the route as the Mediterranean hub for the return leg: Catalan jazz has one of the longest-standing traditions on the Iberian Peninsula. **Jamboree**, in the heart of the Gothic Quarter, has been programming jazz and soul since the 1960s. The **Barcelona International Jazz Festival** (November, since 1966) is one of Spain's oldest festivals, with a programme that mixes international names with the local scene.

**Club:** [Jamboree](https://www.jamboree.cat/) · **Festival:** [Barcelona Jazz Festival](https://www.jazz.barcelona/) (November)

---

### 9. San Sebastián — Jazz by the bay

The **Heineken Jazzaldia** in San Sebastián (July, since 1966) is one of the best-situated jazz festivals in Europe: stages in the Plaza de la Trinidad and the main outdoor venue, with the sea as a backdrop and the Parte Vieja as the setting. It combines major international names with more experimental programming, making San Sebastián an essential summer stop on the route.

**Festival:** [Heineken Jazzaldia](https://www.jazzaldia.eus/) (July)

---

### 10. Rome — Heritage and jazz in the park

**Casa del Jazz**, opened in 2005 in a 1940s villa in the EUR district, is the centre of gravity of Roman jazz: gardens, an auditorium and programming that brings together Italian and international jazz. Rome closes the route with a stop that combines heritage, Mediterranean atmosphere and a nightlife scene that runs very late.

**Club:** [Casa del Jazz](https://www.casadeljazz.com/) · **Festival:** Roma Jazz Festival (November)

## When to travel

**Summer (June–July):** Red Light Jazz Amsterdam (June), Copenhagen Jazz Festival and Jazzaldia (July), Montreux Jazz Festival (July). The concentration of festivals makes summer the best time to combine 3–4 stops on the route.

**Autumn (October–November):** Jazz sur Seine Paris (October), London Jazz Festival, Jazzfest Berlin and Barcelona Jazz Festival (November). Autumn is the other major window — more urban, more club-based, less outdoors.

**Year-round:** Ronnie Scott's, Bimhuis, Porgy & Bess, Jazzhus Montmartre and Jamboree programme almost without a break. Any month is good for visiting the clubs on the route.
