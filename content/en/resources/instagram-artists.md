Instagram remains the reference platform for visual artists, musicians, and any creator whose work is photographable or filmable. Discovery has shifted — Reels have displaced the feed — but the platform is still where programmers, gallery directors, and journalists go to find artists. Understanding how it works prevents wasting time on content that reaches no one.

## How the algorithm works (simplified)

Instagram has four surfaces, each with its own algorithm logic:

**Feed (square / 4:5)**: your work appears to people who already follow you. Organic reach has dropped to 3–8% of your followers. Content that generates interaction (comments and saves — not just likes) gets amplified.

**Reels (9:16 vertical)**: the main discovery surface. Reels are shown to people who don't follow you. A Reel that performs well can multiply by 10 the reach of an equivalent feed post.

**Stories (9:16 vertical, 24h)**: for your existing followers. No discovery potential. It's the channel for authenticity and intimacy — what you show behind the scenes, the process, the day-to-day.

**Explore**: powered by the algorithm. You can't publish directly here. Content that performs well as a Reel or feed post can end up in Explore.

**Safe zone for Stories and Reels**: Stories display the platform interface (avatar, name, progress bar) at the edges. Leave a safety margin of 250px at the top and bottom for text and key elements — otherwise they'll be covered by the UI.

## Content strategy for artists

The biggest mistake: only posting finished work. Finished work is actually what the algorithm rewards least, because it generates little interaction. Process, decisions, mistakes, and surprises spark conversation.

**Content types that work for artists:**

**Creative process** (Stories, Reels): a few-second clip of what you're doing. A brush applying colour, hands working clay, a score taking shape. No editing or explanation needed — process is visually compelling on its own.

**Before/after** (Carousel): the first slide shows the finished piece, the following slides show the process. One of the formats with the highest saves and shares.

**Anecdotes and questions** (Stories): "Which version do you prefer?" "I'm torn between two titles." Direct interaction with your audience builds community.

**Finished work** (Feed): low frequency, high quality. Each feed post of finished work must be impeccable. It's your shop window.

**Behind the scenes** (Stories, Reels): the journey to the venue, load-in, rehearsal, the production meeting. What audiences see when you're not performing.

## Posting in your own language

Instagram does not penalise any language. If you're a Welsh-speaking artist, a Basque musician, or create in any minority or regional language — post in your language without hesitation. Linguistic authenticity is an identity marker that differentiates you. Your audience who shares that language will find you, and authenticity reads globally. For international reach, a sentence in English in the caption complements without replacing.

---

## Recommended frequency

- **Feed**: 3–4 posts/week maximum. Flooding the feed is penalised.
- **Stories**: 2–5 Stories/day when you have genuine content. Don't inflate for the sake of it.
- **Reels**: 2–3 per week if you have filmable process content. One good Reel beats five mediocre ones.

---

## Common mistakes

**Posting without a caption.** Instagram is a search engine. A post without keywords in the description is invisible to new users.

**Too many hashtags.** Three to five specific hashtags (not generic ones like #art or #music with hundreds of millions of results) work better than thirty generic ones.

**Ignoring comments.** Replying to comments within the first two hours amplifies the post's reach. Instagram's algorithm reads fast interaction as a quality signal.

**Deleting posts that underperformed.** A post with low reach does no harm. Deleting posts destabilises your feed and can negatively affect your algorithm history.
