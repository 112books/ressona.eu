The European Union directs hundreds of millions of euros annually to the cultural sector through programmes like Creative Europe, Erasmus+ and the structural funds. The problem isn't that the money doesn't exist — it's that the system is complex, deadlines are demanding and competition is high. But for those who prepare well, the opportunities are real and renewable.

## Creative Europe: the main programme

**[Creative Europe](https://culture.ec.europa.eu/creative-europe)** is the EU's framework programme for culture and creativity. It has two main strands:

**MEDIA** — for audiovisual projects: film, series, video games, television. Supports development, distribution, promotion and training in the audiovisual sector. Primarily aimed at professional production companies.

**Culture** — for multidisciplinary projects with a European dimension. The main calls:

- **Co-operation projects**: grants for projects involving at least three organisations from three European countries. Application deadlines usually open in autumn. Funding can cover 60–80% of the project budget. For dance, music, visual arts, literature or circus companies wanting to build a European network.
- **European Platforms**: for organisations wanting to create stable distribution and exhibition networks at European level.
- **European Literatures** and support for literary translation.

The entry point for artists in Spain is the **[Creative Europe Desk Spain](https://www.creativeeuropemedia.eu/es/)**, which runs information sessions and advises on calls.

## Erasmus+ for arts and culture

**[Erasmus+](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/)** is not only for university students. There are specific lines for professional training in performing arts, music and creative sectors:

- **Mobility for cultural professionals**: individual grants for training, residencies and exchanges in other European countries.
- **Partnerships for cooperation**: for organisations wanting to develop joint educational or training projects.

Management for Spain is through the **[National Erasmus+ Agency SEPIE](https://www.sepie.es/)**, which publishes calls and manages national applications.

## European Cultural Foundation (ECF)

The **[European Cultural Foundation](https://www.culturalfoundation.eu/)** is independent of the EU but one of the main sources of European private cultural funding. It funds projects working on social inclusion, creative freedom and cultural democracy. Access is competitive but accessible for small and medium organisations. Calls vary each year — check their website directly.

## Other European foundations with cultural programmes

- **[Goethe Institut](https://www.goethe.de/en/)**: residency and artistic collaboration programmes with connections to the German and European market.
- **[Institut Français](https://www.institutfrancais.com/)**: support for cultural projects with French or Francophone connections.
- **[Robert Bosch Stiftung](https://www.bosch-stiftung.de/en/)**: grants for cultural exchanges between Germany and other European countries, especially for young artists.
- **[Prince Claus Fund](https://www.princeclausfund.org/)**: funding for artists from cultures facing restrictions or resource scarcity. Based in the Netherlands.

## How to prepare a European application

Three things that cause most applications to fail:

**The European dimension cannot be decorative.** A project that "could be exported" is not the same as a project born from real collaboration with European partners. Creative Europe wants to see structured collaboration built into the design, not added as justification.

**The budget must be realistic and justified.** European evaluators immediately detect inflated or poorly itemised budgets. Every line must have a clear justification.

**Follow-through is as important as the application.** Entities receiving European funding must submit detailed progress reports. If you don't have the administrative capacity, find a partner who does.
