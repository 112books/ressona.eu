Tax is nobody's favourite topic. But it's one of the most important ones to understand, because the consequences of ignoring it arrive with surcharges and penalties.

This isn't tax advice — for that you need an accountant or tax adviser who knows your specific situation. What we offer here is the general framework: how the self-employed (autónomo) regime works for creative activities in Spain, what your obligations are, and which mistakes are most commonly made.

## Registering as self-employed: when it's compulsory

The general rule: if you carry out an economic activity in a habitual, personal and direct way for your own account, you must register as self-employed (RETA — Régimen Especial de Trabajadores Autónomos, Spain's self-employed social security regime) and in the tax census (form 036 or 037) with the Tax Agency.

**"Habitual" is the contentious point.** There's no minimum income threshold in law that defines when an activity is "habitual". The common practical interpretation is that if income from the activity regularly exceeds the monthly national minimum wage, the activity is habitual and registration is required.

A musician who gives two concerts a year probably doesn't need to register. A musician who performs regularly, collects fees, gives lessons and generates recurring income does.

**Dual registration (pluriactividad):** If you have an employment contract and also carry out self-employed artistic work, you can register as self-employed under dual activity status. There are social security contribution reductions available in these cases.

## The self-employed contribution: the income-based system

Since 2023, self-employed contributions in Spain are no longer a fixed flat rate — they're adjusted to net business income. The system works in bands:

| Monthly net income band | Approximate monthly contribution (2025) |
|---|---|
| Up to €670 | ~€230 |
| €670 – €900 | ~€260 |
| €900 – €1,166 | ~€275 |
| €1,166 – €1,300 | ~€291 |
| €1,300 – €1,500 | ~€294 |
| Over €6,000 | ~€590 |

*(Exact figures change annually. Check the current table on the Social Security website.)*

At the start of the year you estimate your income and choose a band. At the end of the year there's a reconciliation: if you earned more, you pay the difference; if you earned less, you get a refund.

**Flat rate for new registrants:** If it's your first time registering (or more than two years have passed since you were last registered), you're entitled to a flat rate of €80/month for the first year. From the second year, the banding system applies normally.

## VAT: when you charge it and when you pay it

The general rule: if you're self-employed and issue invoices, you must charge VAT (IVA — Impuesto sobre el Valor Añadido, Spain's equivalent of VAT, normally at 21%) on the invoices you issue and pay it to the Tax Agency quarterly.

**But there are exceptions for artists:**

- **Live performances:** live performances by artists are VAT-exempt if the artist performs directly without an intermediary. If you perform through a company (promoter, agency), the VAT chain changes.
- **Author's rights managed by SGAE or similar:** public communication and mechanical reproduction rights managed by collective management organisations don't generate VAT directly for the author.
- **Classes and training:** non-regulated teaching activities (workshops, private lessons) do carry VAT at 21%. Regulated and accredited educational activities may be exempt.

Most artists who perform, teach and collect rights income will mix invoices with and without VAT. Precise classification of each income stream is essential.

**Quarterly VAT return (form 303):** each quarter you must declare the difference between the VAT you've charged clients and the VAT you've paid on your professional expenses (input VAT). If you've charged more VAT than you've paid, you pay the difference. If you've paid more VAT than you've charged (unusual), you're entitled to offset it.

## Income tax: withholdings and the annual return

When you issue an invoice as a self-employed professional, you normally apply an **IRPF (Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas — Spanish personal income tax) withholding** that the client deducts and pays directly to the Tax Agency on your behalf. The standard withholding is 15% for professionals (7% during the first two years of activity).

But note: **the withholding is not the final tax.** It's a payment on account. When you file your annual tax return, everything is reconciled: if the total withholdings exceed what you owe based on your overall income, the Tax Agency refunds the difference. If they fall short, you pay the difference.

**Quarterly tax prepayments (form 130):** if you receive income from clients who aren't obliged to withhold from you (individuals, some foreign self-employed, etc.), you must make your own quarterly prepayments on account of IRPF. If 70% or more of your income carries a withholding, you may be exempt from this requirement.

## Common mistakes that cost money

**Not separating personal and professional accounts.** It's not legally required for self-employed individuals to have a separate bank account (unlike limited companies), but mixing personal and professional expenses makes bookkeeping enormously difficult and raises red flags in the event of an inspection.

**Collecting cash payments without invoicing.** A performance fee in cash without an invoice is undeclared income. If the Tax Agency detects it (and they often do, especially when the payer is a company that has declared the expense), the penalties can be disproportionate.

**Underestimating quarterly prepayments.** Many freelance creatives earn irregularly: busy months followed by quiet ones. It's easy to underestimate and face a large bill at annual tax return time. Set aside a percentage of your income periodically for taxes — 25–30% is a conservative estimate.

**Not deregistering when you stop the activity.** If you stop working, deregistering from RETA takes effect almost immediately from the first of the following month. If you don't deregister, you keep paying contributions.
