A casting agent doesn't read your bio. A casting director won't wait while you explain who you are. You have thirty seconds — sixty if you're lucky — for your showreel to convince them you're worth calling in.

The showreel and the photo book are not "optional materials to get around to when you have time". They are the basic professional presentation documents for any actor or actress who wants to be taken seriously by agencies, directors and production companies. Having them done badly is almost worse than not having them at all.

## The showreel: function and structure

A showreel is a presentation video that brings together clips from your best performances. Its function is not to show everything you've done: it is to convince, in the shortest time possible, that you can act and that you are bankable for a certain type of role.

**Ideal length: 90 seconds – 2 minutes.** Any shorter and it looks as though you don't have material. Any longer and most professionals won't make it to the end. If you have enough quality material for something longer, consider editing specific versions for different contexts.

**Structure makes all the difference:**

Your best scene goes first, always. The first fifteen seconds decide whether the person keeps watching. Don't save "the best for last" — in a showreel, the end doesn't exist if the opening seconds don't work.

After the opening, you can alternate pace and register: drama and comedy, close-up and wide shot, different types of roles if you have versatility. Each cut must be clean and earn its place. No filler material.

**What to include:**

- Clips from television series, feature films, short films or professionally recorded theatre
- Scenes from workshops directed by a director who knows what they're doing (not studio improvisations under practice-session lighting)
- If there is dialogue in foreign languages, subtitles in the main language of the market you are targeting

**Technical format:** MP4, minimum 1080p, decent audio quality. A showreel with good material but poor sound is a showreel that doesn't work.

## When you don't have enough material

The most common situation for actors in training or with limited screen experience: you have talent, you have theatre experience, but you don't have quality recorded material. Here is a progressive path forward.

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">01</span>

**Directed practice scenes.** Not just any scenes: scenes filmed with a director who knows what they're doing, under minimum technical conditions (lighting, sound, steady camera). Many professional actors do dedicated scene sessions specifically for their showreel. This is not cheating — it is common sense.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">02</span>

**Theatre filmed with intention.** A 4K mobile phone with good lighting and a tripod can capture useful material from a theatre performance. You don't need television production values: you need a stable image, audible sound and visible action. Many small venues will allow you to record if you ask in advance.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">03</span>

**Short self-produced pieces with a script.** A brief short film, a scene written and filmed expressly for this purpose, with a competent acting partner and a director. Better than nothing, and perfectly valid if the quality is decent. Professionals understand that actors at an early stage need to generate material.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">04</span>

**Short film workshops.** There are training programmes that culminate in the production of a real short film. Alongside the training itself, they generate showreel material in a collaborative context with guaranteed minimum technical standards. Consider it a double investment: training and material.
</div>

</div>

## The photo book

The photo book is your visual CV. It arrives before you do, gets sent by email, and is consulted in agency databases. It must be clear, clean and current.

**Casting photographs (headshots):** Neutral or unobtrusive background, face to camera, clear lighting that doesn't mask your features. The aim is for the director to know exactly who you are: age, physicality, natural expression. No filters, no retouching that alters your actual appearance. If they meet you at the casting and you don't look like your photo, that is a problem.

**Press or character photographs:** More freedom here. Setting, expression, costume that suggests a type of role. Useful for promotion, for festival presentations or for specific projects. They don't replace headshots — they complement them.

**Digital format:** High-resolution JPG (minimum 1500px on the short side), versions in square ratio for social media and agency profiles. Clear file names: `firstname-lastname-casting-01.jpg`, not `IMG_4832.jpg`.

<h3>Showreel with dramatic music laid over everything</h3>
<p>Background music in a showreel is, almost always, a mistake. It covers the sound of the scenes, creates an artificial tone and signals that the editor was compensating for a lack of quality material. If the scenes work, they don't need a soundtrack. If they don't work without one, they shouldn't be in the showreel.</p>

<h3>Material from five years ago that no longer represents you</h3>
<p>If you have changed physically, if you have grown considerably as an actor, or if that clip belongs to an earlier phase of your career, remove it. A showreel must represent who you are now, not who you were. Old material that doesn't fit your present creates confusion rather than helping.</p>

<h3>Photo book with filters or retouching that conceals real physical features</h3>
<p>The photo book is not Instagram. Heavy retouching that alters skin tone, removes distinctive features or reshapes the face is counterproductive: when you arrive at the casting and you don't look like your photographs, the casting agent registers an inconsistency that does not work in your favour.</p>

<h3>No direct contact details or agent link included</h3>
<p>If your showreel circulates independently — sent by email, shared via Vimeo, found by chance — it must contain, at the beginning or end, your full name, email address or website, and your agency's contact details if you have representation. A showreel without contact information is a missed opportunity.</p>

## Professional presentation is an investment, not an expense

The showreel and the photo book should not be made "when there's time and money". They are active working documents. An out-of-date showreel excludes you from circuits you could otherwise access. A poorly made photo book is the first impression that shows how seriously you take your career.
