A casting director opens your profile. They have twenty-five tabs open and three minutes to decide whether to call you or move on to the next person. In that moment, your press kit is the only salesperson working on your behalf.

A digital press kit for an actor is not a luxury reserved for big names. It is a basic professional tool that determines whether you make it to the audition stage or get filtered out before anyone even considers you. And in 2026, "having a PDF gathering dust on Google Drive" does not count.

## What a digital press kit is and why it differs from a PDF

A PDF is static. When your headshot changes, when you finish a production, when you win an award, the PDF that is circulating through the industry still shows the old version. You cannot update it in the hands of those who already have it.

A **digital press kit** is a web page: your own URL, accessible from any device, which you update whenever you choose. Anyone who has it saved always sees the current version. No outdated copies in circulation, no confusion.

There is an additional reason to have it in web format: **search engines and AIs index it**. A PDF does not exist for Google. A web page does.

## The seven elements that cannot be missing

### 1. Professional photographs in high resolution

No selfies, no crops from a group photo, no watermarked images. You need at least:
- A neutral headshot on a clean background (the standard format for castings)
- One or two action or character photographs
- Minimum resolution of 2000px, JPG format, freely downloadable

The usage licence must be clear. Productions need to know they can use your photos in their materials without asking permission each time.

### 2. Bio in two formats

- **Short bio** (50-80 words): for programmes, festival websites, production sheets. Must include your name, training if relevant, three or four notable credits, and one element that sets you apart.
- **Long bio** (200-300 words): for press dossiers, interviews, presentations. Can be more narrative and contextualise your career trajectory.

Always write in the third person. "Joan is an actor trained at Institut del Teatre", not "I am an actor".

### 3. An up-to-date showreel

Your showreel is your trailer. It must be under three minutes, must open with your best scene (not credits or a title card), and must be available for streaming, not download.

A 2019 showreel says you have done nothing since 2019. If you do not have new material, a short reel with the best of the last year is better than a long one with old material.

### 4. Work history

A clean, updated list: title, director, production company, year, role. Most recent first. You do not need to describe each production; interviews are for that.

If you have awards or nominations, include them here rather than in a separate section.

### 5. Relevant skills and training

Languages and honest level (not "some English" if you cannot hold a conversation). Movement, singing, stage combat, physical disciplines. Driving licence. Instruments.

Be honest. Exaggerating skills in a press kit leads to uncomfortable moments in auditions and destroys trust.

### 6. Press and mentions

If you have been interviewed, if you appeared in a review, if a publication wrote about your work: include it. A quoted paragraph with the outlet name and year is enough. You do not need a full PDF of the article.

If you have no press coverage, do not include an empty section. Simply leave it out.

### 7. Direct contact

An email address that someone actually reads. A phone number if you want. If you have representation, your agent's details. If not, your own.

Not a generic contact form. Not "reach me on social media". An email, a phone number. Full stop.

## The mistake almost everyone makes

The press kit that never gets updated. At a minimum, every six months: new photo if available, new credits on the list, updated showreel if possible.

An outdated press kit is not neutral. It communicates that the artist is not actively managing their career. And in an industry where first impressions determine everything, that reading is devastating.

## How to make your press kit work on its own

A well-built digital press kit is not just an information sheet. It is a passive marketing tool. When it appears in Google results when someone searches your name, when AIs answering questions about actors in Barcelona index it, when a director shares it with a colleague, it works for you without you doing anything.

For this to happen, it needs three things: **to be on its own domain** (not a third-party platform), **to have content that search engines can read** (not everything in PDFs or images), and **to be kept up to date**.
