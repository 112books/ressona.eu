Digital distribution has democratised access to global markets: any independent artist can have their music on Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, Tidal and 150 more platforms without needing a record label. But the system has nuances you need to understand to avoid losing money or rights.

## How digital distribution works

You don't upload music directly to Spotify. You do it through a digital distributor (DistroKid, TuneCore, Amuse, CD Baby...) which delivers your audio and metadata to all platforms. In exchange, they charge you an annual fee or a royalty commission.

The flow is:

**Artist → Distributor → Platforms → Listeners → Royalties → Distributor → Artist**

## Main distributors and their models

| Distributor | Model | Commission | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DistroKid** | Annual fee (~£19/year) | 0% | Unlimited releases, very popular |
| **TuneCore** | Per release (~£9 single/year) | 0% | Good for fewer releases |
| **CD Baby** | One-time payment (~£9 single) | 9% | No annual fee, good for occasional releases |
| **Amuse** | Free (basic plan) | 0% or commission | Free plan with limitations |
| **Bandcamp** | Own platform | 10–15% | Direct sales, highly valued by indie artists |

For most independent artists releasing regularly, **DistroKid** or **TuneCore** offer the best value.

## How much do you earn per stream

The reality of streaming royalties disappoints many artists. Approximately:

- **Spotify**: ~€0.003–0.005 per stream
- **Apple Music**: ~€0.007–0.01 per stream
- **Tidal**: ~€0.012 per stream (higher as a HiFi-focused service)
- **YouTube Music**: ~€0.002 per stream

To earn €1,000 from Spotify you need between 200,000 and 333,000 streams. Getting rich from streaming is very difficult unless you have a large and consistent audience.

## Register your music correctly

Each release needs an **ISRC** (international code identifying the recording) and, if you have original compositions, an **ISWC** (code identifying the composition). Distributors assign ISRCs automatically. ISWCs are generated when you register works with your collection society.

Without ISRC, platforms can't accurately count your streams. Without registration with a collection society, you lose public performance royalties.

## Bandcamp as a strategic complement

Bandcamp is different from Spotify: people buy directly, and Bandcamp takes a 10–15% commission. For fans who want to actively support an artist, it's the preferred option. Many independent artists earn more through Bandcamp (with a small but loyal audience) than through Spotify (with many streams but minimal royalties).

On Bandcamp Fridays, the platform waives its commission — the highest-sales day of the year for many independent artists.

## Distribution alone isn't enough

Uploading your music is just the first step. Nobody will listen to your record if they don't know it exists. Streaming works when it's paired with a communication strategy: social media presence, press kit for media, pitching to editorial playlists, and a website that gives context to your work.

Distribution is the tool. Digital identity is what makes the tool actually useful.
