You have music. You have talent. You have gigs. But when someone searches for you on Google, what they find is an outdated Instagram profile, a Facebook page you haven't touched since 2021, and nothing else. Or worse: they find nothing at all.

A musician's digital identity is not a website. It is the sum of everything the world sees when it searches your name: your website, your photographs, your press kit, how Google describes you, how AIs recommend you. When all these elements tell the same story, you become a professional. When they don't, you are invisible.

## Where to start

The most common mistake is trying to do everything at once: website, Instagram, press kit, newsletter. The result is that nothing gets done properly.

The starting point is always **visual identity**: your logo, your colour palette, your communication tone. Not because it is the most urgent thing, but because it is the foundation on which everything else must be built. A website with an improvised logo is like a gig in a venue with no sound system: the content may be excellent, but the perception will be amateur.

With visual identity defined, the second step is the **website**. Not a profile on someone else's platform. A website of your own, with your own domain, that you control. Because platforms change their rules, reduce organic reach, discontinue features. Your own website does not.

## What a musician's website must have

A musician's website does not need to be complex. It needs to be **fast, clear and actionable**. The bookers and journalists who land on your site need to find three things in under ten seconds:

1. **Who you are** — name, musical style, two adjectives that define you
2. **Where they can see you** — upcoming dates, recent videos
3. **How to contact you** — email, form, manager

Everything else is secondary. The complete song catalogue, the full discography since 2010, the photo gallery from every gig: useful, but not what brings work.

## The digital press kit nobody tells you you need

A press kit is the document you send when you want to play a festival, when a journalist wants to interview you, when a venue asks for information. Most musicians have an outdated one sitting in a PDF they haven't touched in years.

A modern **digital press kit** is a web page (or a web document) that includes:
- Short bio (50 words) and long bio (200 words)
- High-resolution photographs with clear usage rights
- Recent videos (streaming, not for download)
- Updated technical rider
- Press clippings and mentions
- Direct contact for the manager or artist

The key is that it must be **easy to update**. A press kit that ages is worse than having none at all, because it communicates neglect.

## Social media: less is more

Instagram is essential for a musician. But Instagram alone is not a digital strategy. Depending on a single platform you do not control is a risk no professional should take.

The rule is simple: **social media brings people to your website, not the other way around**. Your website is your property. Instagram is a distribution channel. When the algorithm changes — and it will — your website is still yours.

Posting on Instagram three times a week without knowing who you are posting for or why is not a strategy: it is noise. Better to post once a week with a clear purpose than to post every day without direction.

## How Google and AIs find you

Search positioning for musicians is different from positioning for a shop or a restaurant. You are not primarily trying to appear when someone searches "jazz musician in Barcelona" (though that is useful). You are trying to ensure that **your own name returns results you control**.

When a booker hears about you and searches your name on Google, they should find: your website, your Instagram page, perhaps an interview. Not a namesake, not a MySpace page from 2008, not nothing.

AIs like ChatGPT or Perplexity are increasingly used to ask for recommendations: "Which jazz musicians play in Barcelona?" or "Who are the best emerging Catalan singer-songwriters?". To appear in these answers, your content must be **citable**: there must be clear statements about who you are, what style you play, where you perform.

## What changes when it all comes together

A musician's digital identity is not a luxury or a "when I find time" priority. It is the difference between a booker who saw you perform being able to find and contact you in five minutes, or giving up and calling another musician who does have their house in order.

Talent is heard. Digital identity is seen. Both things matter.
