Crowdfunding isn't a plan B for when there's no money. Done right, it's a validation tool, a community-building exercise and a pre-sale mechanism that makes it possible to produce projects that would otherwise never happen.

Done badly, it's a campaign that doesn't reach its minimum target, leaves you exhausted and damages your reputation with the people who supported you.

The difference between these two outcomes isn't luck — it's preparation.

## When a campaign makes sense

Crowdfunding works when you already have an audience that follows you and trusts your work. It's not a tool to find new audiences — it's a tool to convert your existing audience into backers for your next project.

**It makes sense if:**
- You've released albums, shows or previous projects that were well received
- You have an active community (mailing list, loyal followers, regular concert attendees)
- The project you want to fund is concrete and has a tangible outcome (album, book, show, tour)
- You can explain the project clearly and emotionally compellingly in under 2 minutes

**It doesn't make sense if:**
- You're just starting out and don't have a support base
- The project is vague or undefined ("I want to record some music")
- You can't dedicate the time and energy an active campaign demands

## The main platforms

| Platform | Model | Reach | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Verkami](https://www.verkami.com/) | All-or-nothing (30 days) | Mainly Catalonia/Spain | Music, film, comics, performance |
| [Goteo](https://goteo.org/) | All-or-nothing + optional extra round | Spain, social and cultural projects | Projects with social impact, open culture |
| [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/) | All-or-nothing | International (English-dominant) | Music, film, games, technology |
| [Indiegogo](https://www.indiegogo.com/) | All-or-nothing or flexible | International | More flexible, less organic community |

For cultural projects in Catalonia, **Verkami** is the local platform with the largest dedicated arts and culture community. Goteo is worth considering if the project has a social dimension or you want to raise funding in two rounds (community + institutional).

## The structure of a campaign that works

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">01</span>

**Define the minimum viable, not the maximum desired.** The minimum target is what you need to produce the project in its essential version. Don't include ideal marketing, a full tour or a professional music video if it's not essential to the core product. People fund the project, not your ideal budget.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">02</span>

**Design rewards carefully.** Rewards must have real value for backers — not just for you. Typical tiers: digital access (€5–10), physical copy (€20–30), exclusive experience (€50–100), patronage with special recognition (€200+). Avoid rewards that generate disproportionate work: if you hit double your target, you may have to handwrite 200 letters.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">03</span>

**The video is 70% of the campaign.** A 90–120 second video explaining who you are, what you want to create and why it matters. You don't need professional production — you need authenticity and clarity. Many campaigns succeed with well-edited phone footage. The quality of the message matters more than the quality of the image.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">04</span>

**Prepare the launch like an opening night.** The first three days determine whether the campaign reaches 30–40% of the target, which is what builds the sense of momentum. You need your support network mobilised before you go live: talk to trusted people, let them know the campaign is coming, ask them to back it on day one.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">05</span>

**Communicate throughout the campaign, not just at the beginning and end.** Weekly updates (or every 3–4 days) keep the campaign visible and give people reasons to share it. Tell the story: production decisions, guest musicians, first rehearsal, cover design test. The campaign is the project's first public narrative.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">06</span>

**Pay attention to post-campaign management.** If you hit your target, the real work begins: producing and delivering rewards on the timelines you promised. Delays in fulfilment destroy the trust you've built. Budget realistic production time and communicate transparently if there are problems.
</div>

</div>

<div>
<h3>Target set too high</h3>
<p>A €10,000 target for a debut album from an emerging artist without an established community is very difficult to reach. If you don't hit the minimum, you receive nothing (in all-or-nothing models) and the public failure is visible. Better to set an achievable target and exceed it.</p>
</div>
</div>

<span class="resource-checklist__icon" aria-hidden="true">

<div>
<h3>Trusting the platform to find you backers</h3>
<p>Crowdfunding platforms don't work like marketplaces where people browse looking for projects. The vast majority of backers come from your own network. The platform is the payment infrastructure and visibility layer — not the discovery channel.</p>
</div>

<div>
<h3>Not preparing your support network in advance</h3>
<p>Launching the campaign and hoping people will find it is the surest way to fail. You need to mobilise your network in the first few days. Talk personally to the 20–30 people you know will support you, explain the project to them directly, and ask them to act on day one.</p>
</div>

<h3>Impossible or miscalculated rewards</h3>
<p>Offering "an acoustic concert at your home" for €50 without calculating the time and logistical cost can seem charming but can be financially ruinous if 40 people choose that reward. Every reward must have its production and fulfilment cost calculated with a margin.</p>

## Crowdfunding and taxes

One point many artists overlook: crowdfunding money is taxable. Generally, if you're registered as self-employed (autónomo), campaign income is incorporated into your business income. If you're not registered, you may still have an obligation to declare it as irregular income.

Consult an accountant or tax adviser before launching the campaign to understand how to declare the income and whether the platform will issue you a certificate for income tax (IRPF — Spain's personal income tax) purposes.

## Crowdfunding as the first narrative of your project

Beyond funding, a well-run campaign builds the project's first audience. Backers don't just pay — they often become the first advocates, the ones who talk about the project in their circles, the ones who show up on opening night.

Treat the campaign as the first chapter of your project's public story, not as an administrative step to secure funding.
