The short answer: **it depends**. LinkedIn isn't Instagram or Spotify. But for some artist profiles, it's a tool that shouldn't be ignored.

<h3>Artists who work in B2B contexts</h3>
<p>If you offer services as a musician for corporate events, design installations for business spaces, run creativity workshops or do artistic coaching, your potential client is a company and LinkedIn is where they are. Ignoring it would be a mistake.</p>

## Who probably doesn't need it

If you're an independent musician whose income depends on performing for general audiences and whose growth relies on fans and popular festival programmers, your social media time will be far better spent on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. LinkedIn is not where your audience lives.

## How to build a good profile if you decide to be there

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">01</span>

**Professional photo consistent with your visual identity.** Same (or same style) as what you use on other platforms. LinkedIn is formal but you don't have to look like an accountant — you can maintain your artistic aesthetic.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">02</span>

**Clear and specific headline.** Not just "Artist". Instead: "Composer and cellist | Contemporary music and film | Barcelona". Explain what you do in one line.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">03</span>

**Summary that explains your value, not your history.** The LinkedIn summary isn't a CV — it's a pitch. What can you do for whoever's reading it? What's your current project? What do you want to be contacted for?
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">04</span>

**Relevant experience with links.** Add links to videos, websites, press. LinkedIn is one of the few networks that allows links directly in posts and profiles without penalising reach.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">05</span>

**Value content, not promotion.** If you post on LinkedIn, talk about the creative process, industry challenges, lessons learned. Content that teaches or provokes thought performs far better than concert announcements.
</div>

</div>

## The golden rule

You don't need to be on every network. You need to be done well wherever you are. A LinkedIn profile abandoned for 3 years is worse than not having one.

If you decide to be on LinkedIn, keep it updated, connect with relevant people in your field and post consistently (once a week or twice a month is enough to maintain a presence without it becoming another job).

## LinkedIn and SEO

One thing many people overlook: your LinkedIn profile appears on Google. When someone searches your name, your LinkedIn profile often appears on the first page. This means that even if you don't use it actively, it's worth having it updated and professional — because it's part of your digital presence, whether you see it or not.
