Online music education has matured significantly since COVID. It is no longer a substitute: some of the world's best schools now offer complete programmes online, with exceptionally high-level teachers and genuine academic recognition. The problem is that the offer is enormous and quality wildly uneven — from serious courses taught by professional musicians to amateur tutorials packaged to look like paid courses.

Most of the platforms and schools in this guide are natively in English, which makes them highly accessible for international musicians. For some platforms (notably Berklee Online and several Coursera courses), Spanish-language content is available, though English versions tend to be more complete. What matters is matching the platform to what you actually need — a recognised qualification, real teacher feedback, instrument-specific content, or structured self-study on a budget. These are different requirements with different answers.

## Schools with recognised qualifications

**[Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/)** is the online arm of Berklee College of Music in Boston, one of the world's most prestigious institutions for jazz and popular music. It offers degrees, master's programmes, and certificates in composition, music production, jazz, pop, guitar, piano, voice, songwriting, and film scoring. Tuition is high — comparable to a US private university — but this is the global reference for higher music education online. Scholarships and payment plans are available. Berklee Online offers some courses in Spanish through specific partnerships. If you need an internationally recognised qualification in popular music, production, or composition, this is the benchmark.

**[BIMM Online](https://www.bimm.ac.uk/)** — British and Irish Modern Music Institute. Degrees in modern music performance, production, and music business with UK academic recognition. More affordable than Berklee and with a European focus. A strong option for those wanting a recognised UK qualification in modern music.

**[Musicians Institute Online](https://mi.edu/)** in Los Angeles offers associate degrees and individual courses in popular music, rock, jazz, and music production. Strong in guitar, bass, and drums. Less internationally known than Berklee but with excellent faculty and very practical programmes.

**[ABRSM](https://www.abrsm.org/)** (Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music) is not an online school but an internationally recognised system of instrumental examinations. You can prepare for any grade (1 through 8) with any teacher or independently, then sit the exam in person at an authorised centre worldwide. Widely used for certification of both classical and popular instrument players. The recognition is genuinely global and well understood by conservatories, music schools, and employers in the music industry.

## Platforms for one-to-one lessons with real teachers

**[ArtistWorks](https://artistworks.com/)** uses a distinctive model: you record a video of your practice — an exercise, a passage, a piece — and your teacher, a professional of the highest level (Tommy Emmanuel for acoustic guitar, Nathan East for bass, Darol Anger for jazz violin), gives you personalised video feedback. This is not a recorded course: it is real tutoring. Highly effective for instruments where visual feedback is critical and where access to world-class teachers would otherwise be impossible or prohibitively expensive.

**[Lessonface](https://www.lessonface.com/)** is a live one-to-one teacher platform with a strong focus on unusual and specialist instruments — Middle Eastern instruments, early music, instruments from various world traditions. Quality is high because the review system is transparent and the teacher pool is carefully curated. One of the best options for less common instruments or highly specialised musical styles.

**[TakeLessons](https://takelessons.com/)** is the largest online private lesson platform. Very broad in scope, variable in quality, but with student reviews available for each teacher. Useful for finding teachers for conventional instruments at reasonable prices, with flexibility in scheduling.

## Instrument-specific platforms

**[JamPlay](https://www.jamplay.com/)** — Guitar. Extensive course library by style and level: acoustic, electric, blues, country, jazz, classical. One of the best structured platforms for guitar with clear progression paths.

**[Drumeo](https://www.drumeo.com/)** — Drums. The best online platform for drums, without serious competition. World-class teachers (Jojo Mayer, Mike Johnston, others), content structured by level and style, active community. If you play drums, this is the reference.

**[Pianote](https://www.pianote.com/)** and **[Playground Sessions](https://www.playgroundsessions.com/)** — Piano. Pianote has better content for complete beginners and self-teaching adults. Playground Sessions has direct MIDI keyboard integration and a gamification approach that works particularly well for sight-reading practice.

**[Learn Jazz Standards](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/)** — Jazz for all instruments. Theory, standards, improvisation, transcriptions, podcasts. A substantial portion of the content is free. The premium content is excellent value for what is offered. Language: English, but the musical content is universal.

**[SingWise](https://www.singwise.com/)** — Voice and vocal technique. One of the few genuinely serious online resources on vocal technique for singers across all styles. Analytically rigorous and grounded in evidence-based vocal pedagogy.

## Classical and early music online

For classical music, serious online options mix with open academic resources. The **Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique et de Danse de Paris (CNSMDP)** publishes open courses via [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/) on music theory and analysis. The quality is that of a real university course.

Craig Wright's **"Listening to Music"** course at **[Open Yale Courses](https://oyc.yale.edu/)** is free and is the best introductory course on Western classical music available online. Twenty-two video lectures, an exceptional teacher, completely open. Ideal for musicians who want to understand the tradition they are working in or alongside.

For early music specifically, the **[Early Music Online YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@EarlyMusicOnline)** archives reference performances with scores. Not a course, but a unique resource for those studying historicist performance practice.

## Music production

**[Splice Learn](https://splice.com/learn)** integrates music production education directly with the Splice sample and sound platform. Courses on DAWs (Ableton, Logic, FL Studio), synthesis, and sound design. Very practical and current.

**[Point Blank Music School Online](https://www.pointblankmusicschool.com/)**, based in London and Los Angeles, is one of the most respected schools for electronic music production and DJing in the industry. Its online programmes are well regarded for those wanting to enter the electronic music sector.

**[Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/)** and **[edX](https://www.edx.org/)** aggregate courses from multiple universities — including Berklee — on music theory, history, music appreciation, and production. Most can be audited for free. Quality varies considerably, but specific courses (Berklee Music Theory, University of Michigan Music Theory) are genuinely excellent. Several Coursera music courses are available in Spanish.

## How to choose: four questions

**Do you need a recognised qualification?** If yes: Berklee Online, BIMM, or ABRSM. No other platform on this list offers qualifications with genuine academic or institutional recognition.

**Do you want real feedback from a real teacher?** ArtistWorks for instruments (video feedback from professional teachers). TakeLessons or Lessonface for live one-to-one lessons. Recorded-content platforms (JamPlay, Drumeo, Pianote) do not offer personal feedback.

**Are you looking for content on a specific instrument?** JamPlay for guitar, Drumeo for drums, Pianote or Playground for piano, Learn Jazz Standards for jazz, SingWise for voice. For less common instruments, Lessonface is the most versatile option.

**Is budget a constraint?** YouTube + Learn Jazz Standards (largely free) + Open Yale Courses + Coursera audited is a combination that costs nothing and covers theory, styles, and instruments to a real level. No qualification, no feedback — but as a structured self-study starting point, it is genuinely useful and more complete than many paid options.
