Most shows that don't fill the venue fail not because of quality, but because of visibility. The problem rarely starts the week before — it starts two months before, when there's still time to build an audience and activate the right channels.

This article lays out the minimum communication framework for any artistic event: concert, theatre premiere, exhibition, dance performance or album launch. No PR agency needed — just structure and consistency.

---

## The minimum calendar

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">−8</span>

**Create your base materials.** Before any communication, you need the foundations: professional photos (or at least good ones), a press release, a 100-word bio and a 50-word event description. Also the graphic visual or poster. Without these materials, you can't contact media or send anything useful.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">−6</span>

**Send the press release to media.** Culture journalists, programmers and specialist blogs work weeks in advance. Sending something three days before the event won't get you coverage. Six weeks out is the minimum to appear in a magazine, programme or blog post. Make a list of relevant media in your discipline and send a personalised email to each one.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">−4</span>

**Start social media communication.** First post: date and venue announcement. From there, build progressively — rehearsal process, making-of, the people involved, your motivation. Not advertising: storytelling. People buy tickets to experiences they feel connected to, not to posters.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">−2</span>

**Activate your personal network.** Direct message — not broadcast — to people you know will come. Ask them to share, not to buy. A genuine recommendation from someone people trust is worth more than any paid ad. This is also the moment to confirm tickets and remind programmers or curators who expressed interest.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">−1</span>

**Countdown content.** Last reminder on social media, daily stories the final week, email to your mailing list if you have one. If you got any press coverage, share it now — it builds credibility and creates social proof. This is the moment for "there are still seats" or "last tickets" if that's true.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">Day</span>

**Document everything.** Hire a photographer or videographer, or at minimum record in 4K with your phone. The content from the event is the best material for the next one. A good full-house photo is worth six months of storytelling. Capture the audience, the space, the backstage, the details.

</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">+1</span>

**Post-event communication.** Photos and video on social media, public thanks to the team and venue, sharing published press. Post-event builds credibility for the next date. People who didn't come now know you exist. People who did come feel recognised.

</div>

</div>

## Press release: minimum structure

A press release isn't a literary text — it's a functional document. Editors need to extract information quickly. This is the minimum structure:

1. **Headline:** who, what, when, where. Maximum 15 words.
2. **Lead:** the first paragraph answers who the artist is, what the event is, and why it matters.
3. **Body:** show description, project context, technique or team if relevant.
4. **Short bio:** 3–5 lines about the artist or company.
5. **Practical data:** date, time, venue, ticket price, booking link.
6. **Press contact:** name, email and phone number.

Maximum one page. Culture editors receive dozens of press releases a week — the short, clear and complete ones get used.

<div class="resource-checklist__text"><strong>Running paid ads without any organic content.</strong> An Instagram ad pointing to an empty profile doesn't convert. Ads amplify what already works — they don't replace a communication strategy.</div>

<div class="resource-checklist__item">
<span class="resource-checklist__icon resource-checklist__icon--no"><svg width="28" height="28" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.2" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><line x1="18" y1="6" x2="6" y2="18"/><line x1="6" y1="6" x2="18" y2="18"/></svg></span>

## Minimum tools

- **Design:** [Canva](https://www.canva.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} for quick materials. Ressona works with [Pocallum](https://pocallum.cat){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} for professional graphic image and photography.
- **Email:** [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} (free up to 500 contacts) or [Brevo](https://www.brevo.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} for managing your mailing list.
- **Social management:** [Meta Business Suite](https://business.facebook.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} to post simultaneously on Instagram and Facebook. [Buffer](https://buffer.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} or [Later](https://later.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} for scheduling content in advance.
- **Web:** An event page or section on your website with all the information in one place. People need one link to share — not a copy-paste of details in every story.
