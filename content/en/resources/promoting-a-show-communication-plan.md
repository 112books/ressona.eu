Most shows don't fail because of lack of quality. They fail because of lack of visibility. And most visibility problems aren't solved the week before the premiere: they're created two months earlier, when nothing has been done.

Here's the minimum communication plan for any artistic event: concert, theatre premiere, exhibition, dance show, album launch. The plan assumes you have no budget for a PR agency and that you're doing it yourself.

## The minimum calendar

<div class="resource-steps">

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">8 weeks before</span>

**Create the base materials.** Show or artist photography (if you don't have any, now is the time to arrange it). Copy: press release, short bio (100 words), event description (50 words). Graphic image: poster or visual for social media. Without these elements, everything that follows is improvisation.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">6 weeks before</span>

**Send the press release to media outlets.** Specialist arts and culture media work weeks ahead. A press release sent the week of the event arrives too late. Contact list: local and national culture journalists, listings editors, specialist blogs and channels.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">4 weeks before</span>

**Start social media communication.** First post: announcement of date and venue. Progressive content over the 4 weeks: rehearsal process, making-of, people involved, motivation behind the project. This isn't advertising — it's building a narrative that makes people want to come.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">2 weeks before</span>

**Activate your personal network.** Direct message (not broadcast) to the people you know will come: friends, colleagues, peers. Ask them to share, not to buy. People buy through recommendation, not advertising.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">1 week before</span>

**Countdown content.** Final reminder on social media. Daily story. If you have a mailing list: send an email. If you have confirmed press coverage: share the mentions on your channels.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">The day</span>

**Document everything.** Photographer or video of the event (minimum: mobile in 4K). The event content is your best material for the next promotion. A good photo of a full house is worth six months of storytelling.
</div>

<div class="resource-steps__item">
<span class="resource-steps__num">The following week</span>

**Post-event communication.** Photos and video from the event on social media. Public acknowledgements. If there's press coverage, share it. Post-event content builds credibility for the next date.
</div>

</div>

## The press release: minimum structure

A press release for a cultural event is not advertising copy. It's journalistic information that an editor needs to be able to use directly.

**Structure:**
1. **Headline** — who, what, when and where (maximum 15 words)
2. **Lead** — the first paragraph answers: who is the artist, what is the event, why does it matter
3. **Body** — description of the show, project context, technique or team if relevant
4. **Short bio** — 3–5 lines about the artist or company
5. **Practical details** — date, time, venue, price, booking
6. **Press contact** — name, email, phone number

Don't go beyond one page. A culture editor receives dozens of press releases a week: the one that's short, clear and contains everything needed is the one that gets used.

<h3>Posting on social media on the day of the event</h3>
<p>On social media, content published today doesn't reach everyone today. Algorithms distribute content progressively. A post published on the day of the event hasn't been seen in time for someone to decide to buy a ticket.</p>
</div>

<div>
<h3>Paid advertising without organic content</h3>
<p>An Instagram ad that leads to an empty profile or one with no recent activity doesn't convert. Advertising amplifies what's already working; it doesn't replace the groundwork.</p>

<h3>Not having a mailing list</h3>
<p>Social networks change their algorithm, disappear, or suspend your account. Your mailing list belongs to you. A list of 300 people who know you is worth more than 3,000 cold followers.</p>

<div>
<h3>Publishing the poster with no other content</h3>
<p>The poster says WHAT. People need to know WHY. Why this show? Why now? Why should I come? The poster is the end point of a campaign, not the starting point.</p>

## Minimum tools needed

- **Design**: Canva for quick materials if you don't have a designer; at Pocallum they work with artists who need professional-quality graphic identity and photography
- **Email**: Mailchimp (free up to 500 contacts) or Brevo for your mailing list
- **Social media management**: Meta Business Suite for simultaneous Instagram/Facebook; Buffer or Later for scheduling
- **Website**: an event page or a section on your website with all the information in one place — people need to be able to share a single link
