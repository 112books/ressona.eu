A casting agent doesn't read your bio. A casting director doesn't wait for you to explain who you are. You have thirty seconds — sixty if you're lucky — for your showreel to convince them you're worth calling in.

The showreel (video summary of best performances) and the photographic book (album of professional photos) are not "optional materials for when you get round to it". They are the basic professional presentation documents for any actor or actress who wants to be taken seriously by agencies, directors and production companies. Having them done badly is almost worse than not having them.

## The showreel: function and structure

**Ideal length: 90 seconds – 2 minutes.** Below that, seems like no material. Above that, most professionals don't reach the end. If you have quality material for longer, consider edited versions for specific contexts.

**Structure matters.** Best scene always first. The first fifteen seconds decide if they keep watching. Don't save "the best for last" — in a showreel, the end doesn't exist if the first seconds don't work.

After the opening, alternate rhythm and register: drama and comedy, close-up and wide shot, different role types if you have range. Each cut must be clean and have a reason to be there. No filler.

**What to include:**

- Fragments from TV series, feature films, short films or professionally recorded theatre
- Scenes from workshops directed by a director with clear artistic vision (not studio improvisations with practical lighting)
- If there's dialogue in foreign languages, subtitles in the primary language of the market you're targeting

**Technical format:** MP4, minimum 1080p, decent audio quality. A showreel with good material but poor audio is a showreel that doesn't work.

## The photographic book

The book is your visual CV. It arrives before you do, gets sent by email, consulted in agency databases. Must be clear, clean and current.

**Casting photos (headshots):** Neutral or discreet background, face to camera, clear light that doesn't mask your features. The goal: the director knows exactly what you look like — age, physique, natural expression. No filters, no retouching that changes your real appearance. If they get to the casting and you don't look like the photo, that's a problem.

**Press or character photos:** More freedom here. Setting, expression, costume that suggests a role type. Useful for promotion, festival presentations or specific projects. Don't replace headshots, complement them.

**Digital format:** JPG high resolution (minimum 1500px short side), versions in square ratio for social media and agency profiles. Clear file names: `firstname-lastname-casting-01.jpg`, not `IMG_4832.jpg`.

## Ready to work with

The showreel and book must not be done "when there's time and money". They are active working documents. An outdated showreel excludes you from circuits you could access. A badly done book is the first impression that shows how seriously you take your career.
