There are two ways to travel with jazz. The **musician's** way: looking for places to play, for connections with local scenes, for the Thursday jam session to stay sharp and cover the rent. And the **enthusiast's** way: organising a holiday around a festival, hunting down that intimate club on Europe's best lists, arriving in a new city with a plan for the evening.

This guide is for both. The starting point is **Barcelona and Catalonia** — with the Jamboree, the JazzSí, the Voll-Damm Festival, Jazz I Am, VijazZ in Vilafranca and a blues and harmonica festival scene unmatched in the rest of Spain — and from here the route extends across Europe.

For musicians: you'll find clubs with regular programming, festivals that welcome spontaneous proposals, and open jam sessions where you can introduce yourself and play. Europe is a real circuit: thousands of musicians travel it every year in the wake of the jazz world. With the right network, you can make a living.

For enthusiasts: you'll find the venues that define each city's scene, the festivals worth a flight, and the cities where jazz is not a tourist attraction but a daily practice.

### Barcelona

**Jamboree** · Plaça Reial 17
[jamboreejazz.com](https://jamboreejazz.com/en/) — Spain's oldest jazz club (1960). Underground vaulted room, two sets a day year-round. Chet Baker, Art Farmer and Ornette Coleman. For musicians: the venue that puts Barcelona on the circuit map.

**Harlem Jazz Club** · C/ de la Comtessa de Sobradiel 8
[harlemjazzclub.es](http://www.harlemjazzclub.es/) — Legendary, in the Gothic Quarter. Jazz, funk and soul almost every night. Capacity 300, dance floor. Affordable entry.

**JazzSí Club** · C/ de Requesens 2
[jazzsi.com](https://www.jazzsi.com/) — Linked to the Taller de Músics. Jazz, blues, flamenco, Cuban jazz. Jam sessions at weekends. A key reference for musicians settling in the city.

**JazzMan** · C/ de Roger de Flor 238
[jazzmanbcn.com](https://jazzmanbcn.com/) — Since 1979, a small club in the Eixample. Jazz, soul and blues. Vinyl sessions on Thursdays.

**[Pipa Club](https://pipaclub.es/)** · Plaça Reial 3 (2nd floor) — Members and guests only. Intimate 1920s atmosphere. Gypsy jazz and swing on Thursdays.

### Madrid

**Café Central** · Plaza del Ángel 10
[cafecentralmadrid.com](https://www.cafecentralmadrid.com/) — Madrid's most iconic jazz club, over 40 years. Concerts every night. Pedro Iturralde, George Adams, Chano Domínguez.

**Sala Clamores** · C/ de Alburquerque 14
[clamores.es](https://www.clamores.es/) — Pioneer since the 1980s. Excellent acoustics, carefully curated programming. Terence Blanchard, Joe Henderson.

**Café Berlín** · C/ Costanilla de los Ángeles 20
[cafeberlin.es](https://www.cafeberlin.es/) — ~500 concerts/year. Jazz, flamenco, funk, soul, folk. Jorge Pardo, Avishai Cohen.

**El Intruso** · C/ Augusto Figueroa 3
[elintruso.com](https://www.elintruso.com/) — At the heart of Chueca. Two benchmark weekly jam sessions: Latin Jam and Ocelot Jam. **For musicians: essential.**

**MOE** · C/ Alberto Alcocer 32 — Since 1999. Jam session every Wednesday. Jerry Gonzalez and Jorge Pardo.

**Populart** · C/ del Huertas 22
[populart.es](https://www.populart.es/) — Classic jazz every night. Free entry.

**Tempo Audiophile Club** · C/ Duque de Osuna 8 — Audiophile bar (inspired by Japanese bars of the 1950s), immersive jazz concerts in the basement.

**Big Mama Ballroom** · C/ de Bernardino Obregón 3
[bigmamaballroom.com](https://www.bigmamaballroom.com/) — Swing in Madrid. Live traditional jazz and dancing. Unique in Europe for what it offers.

### Festivals in Catalonia and Spain

**Jazz I Am**
[jazziam.barcelona](https://jazziam.barcelona/ca/) — March. At El Molino (since 2026). A festival that changes format every year: new venues, new ideas. Emerging artists alongside established names, without hierarchy. Jazz from the Taller de Músics in the room it deserves.

**VijazZ — Jazz Vilafranca del Penedès**
[vijazz.cat](https://vijazz.cat) — July. Intimate festival in Vilafranca del Penedès (Alt Penedès). European and American musicians face to face with the audience, no distances. Joe Lovano, Makaya McCraven and Avishai Cohen have performed here.

**Voll-Damm Festival Internacional de Jazz de Barcelona**
[voll-dammfestivaldejazz.com](https://www.voll-dammfestivaldejazz.com/) — November. Founded in 1966, one of the oldest jazz festivals in the world. Palau de la Música Catalana, Barts, Apolo, Luz de Gas and over 100 concerts in two weeks. **For touring musicians: the moment when Barcelona concentrates the highest density of industry professionals.**

**Sax-o-Rama**
— La Paloma, Barcelona. International saxophone festival created by Dani Nel·lo. Musicians from Japan, the Caribbean and Spain with the Barcelona Big Blues Band as the common thread. "Demanding, generous and uncompromising."

**Festival de Jazz de Terrassa**
[jazzterrassa.org](https://www.jazzterrassa.org/) — March. Since 1980, one of the benchmark festivals on the Catalan scene. 30+ concerts over ten days. Nova Jazz Cava as the epicentre, with programming in the streets and singular spaces across the city.

**Festival de Blues de Barcelona**
[festivalbluesbarcelona.com](https://www.festivalbluesbarcelona.com) — July. 22+ editions, organised from Nou Barris. National and international blues, closing jam sessions. Free entry.

**Pícnic de Blues**
— May. Parc Central de Nou Barris. Free, neighbourhood, no pretensions. Blues on the grass as a season warm-up. Organised by Capibola Blues since 2013.

**Clot amb Blues**
— February and November. El Clot, Barcelona. Blues in neighbourhood spaces: squares, local venues, community facilities. "Small in size, big in content."

**Calella Harmònica Festival**
— September. Calella (Maresme). Concerts, masterclasses for all levels and photography exhibitions. Blues, jazz, bluegrass and folk with the harmonica as the star.

**Festival de Jazz de Girona**
[jazzgirona.cat](https://jazzgirona.cat/) — Summer. Jazz in the Old Quarter, concerts in Romanesque courtyards and churches. Free entry to most concerts.

## For musicians: how to break into the circuit

**Key open jam sessions:**
- Paris: Le Duc des Lombards (Fri–Sat 23:30) · 38Riv (Mon–Sat 23:00)
- Madrid: El Intruso (Latin Jam + Ocelot Jam) · MOE (Wednesdays)
- Amsterdam: Bimhuis (Tuesdays, free)
- Berlin: Zig Zag (Tuesdays)
- Zurich: JazzBaragge (Wednesdays)
- Donostia: Be Bop (festival nights and weekends)

**Tips for touring musicians:**
1. **Bring digital materials**: EPK in PDF, audio/video accessible from your phone. Many clubs programme at the last minute to cover cancellations. If you don't have a professional web presence, the [Ressona Starter Pack](/en/start/) gets you up and running in 48 hours.
2. **Arrive early to the jam session**: the first ones to play tend to be best positioned for a conversation with the programmer.
3. **Local networks**: every scene has specific Facebook groups or forums. Search "jazz musicians [city]" + the local association networks (Assejazz in Seville, Bilbaina Jazz Club in Bilbao).
4. **High season**: June–August for festivals; September–November for clubs. Summer is festival time, autumn is club time.
5. **Residencies and grants**: INAEM (Spain), ICEC (Catalonia), Centre national de la musique (France) and Musikfonds (Germany) offer touring support. Look for calls 6 months in advance.
