Ressona for visual artists: professional digital presence

Painting, sculpture, art photography, installation or digital art: digital portfolio, EPK, website and visual identity for visual artists.

Musicians Actors & Actresses Dance Circus Visual Artists Emerging Artists

For a visual artist, the work is the argument. But without a digital presence that shows it well, the work never reaches the people who make decisions — gallery owners, curators, exhibition commissioners, collectors, grant juries. Art that isn’t seen doesn’t exist in the circuits that matter.

The paradox is that many artists with excellent work lose opportunities for reasons that have nothing to do with quality: an outdated PDF sent by email, a website that hasn’t been updated in three years, or a photo of the work taken on a phone in poor lighting conditions.

Who is looking for you

Every real opportunity comes with someone actively searching for specific information. What they find — or don’t find — determines whether they move to the next stage or move on to the next name on the list.

Gallery owners and art spaces

They want a navigable digital portfolio, not a PDF attachment. They need to see complete series, dimensions, technique, year and reference prices. If they have to write to ask for basic information, most won't bother. The tool that solves this: a professional digital portfolio.

Exhibition commissioners and curators

They need downloadable high-resolution artwork images, a clear artistic statement and an exhibition CV. The statement is the first filter: if it doesn't quickly communicate what your project is and what you're interested in exploring, the conversation never starts. The tool: a structured artistic dossier with statement, images and EPK for visual arts.

Grant and residency juries

They evaluate dossiers in very little time. They need: structured artistic CV, dossier of selected works with technical sheets, statement adapted to the specific call, and a project proposal. Format and visual coherence also count — a poorly laid-out dossier loses points before anyone reads it. Read: dossier for grants and cultural subsidies.

Buyers and collectors

They look for a clear website where they can see available work, understand pricing and make direct contact. A collector who can't find out whether a piece has a gallery price or a direct sale price, or who can't find how to contact you, won't buy. The tool: your own website with an available works section and direct contact form.

The specific tools

The visual arts circuit has its own conventions. It’s not the same as the music world or the performing arts. The tools need to respond to this specific logic.

01

Digital portfolio. Not a PDF — an updatable website. It should be organised by series, with dates, dimensions, technique and availability for each piece. The portfolio is the first impression and the permanent reference document — it must be mobile-friendly and load quickly. Ressona works with LinuxBCN to build artist portfolios optimised for search engines and easy to maintain.

02

Artistic dossier and EPK. The document you present to galleries, festivals, residencies and open calls. It includes: artistic statement (short and long version), exhibition and education CV, selected works with technical sheets and high-resolution images, previous exhibitions and, where relevant, awards and recognition. Read the guide: EPK for visual arts.

03

Artwork photography. A bad photo destroys excellent work. Artwork images must be taken with appropriate lighting, correct angle and sufficient resolution for editorial use and print. Ressona works with Pocallum for artwork documentation sessions for visual artists. Read: professional photos for artists.

04

Coherent visual identity. Your artistic name needs to be recognisable: logotype or personal brand, colour palette, typography. When someone receives your dossier, visits your website and finds you on Instagram, they should have the same visual experience. Coherence projects professionalism without needing to explain it. Ressona works with Pocallum for visual identity design for artists.

05

Website with direct contact. The portfolio must include a clear way for galleries, collectors and institutions to get in touch. A specific form for commercial enquiries is different from a generic contact form. Context matters: the person contacting you needs to feel they’re speaking with someone who understands the difference between a collector enquiry and an exhibition proposal.

Presentation is part of the criteria

The best work in the world presented with a phone photo and a 2019 PDF won't win a grant. The jury evaluates everything they receive — including how it is presented. Visual presentation is part of the criteria, explicitly or not.
