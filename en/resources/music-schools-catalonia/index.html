Music schools in Catalonia: a guide by style and instrument

Conservatories, municipal music schools, jazz and modern music centres. A practical guide to finding where to study music in Catalonia based on your style and level.

3 July 2026 · 7 min read

TrainingMusicCataloniaConservatories

Catalonia has a dense and diverse network of music schools — from top-level conservatories to municipal schools, modern music centres, and spaces specialising in jazz, traditional music, or flamenco. The difficulty is not that there is nowhere to study: it is knowing where to study based on what you are actually looking for.

A practical note for international musicians: most institutions in the Catalan public system teach in Catalan and Spanish. Private schools and some programmes at Taller de Músics offer courses in English, and online global platforms (covered in our separate guide) are fully in English. If language is a barrier, that guide is a good starting point alongside this one.

How the Catalan music education system works

The official music education system in Catalonia follows three levels:

Elementary education — First stage, typically for ages 6–12, though many centres also accept adults. Offered at municipal music schools and approved private centres. The goal is a first serious engagement with an instrument and basic musical language. This level does not directly grant access to professional studies, but prepares students for the entrance exams.

Professional education — Equivalent in length and academic standing to secondary school (bachillerato). Six years of study. Taught at professional conservatories. Admission is by exam (instrument and theory). The professional degree certificate gives access to higher music education.

Higher education — The higher music degree is equivalent to a university degree. In Catalonia, only ESMUC and the Conservatori Superior Municipal de Música de Barcelona offer it. Highly competitive. Admission is by audition and theoretical exams.

Alongside official qualifications, there is a large ecosystem of non-regulated training — jazz schools, summer courses, masterclasses, online platforms — which is often more directly useful for working musicians. The two tracks coexist and complement each other.

Classical and academic music

ESMUC — Escola Superior de Música de Catalunya is the reference centre for higher music education in Catalonia. Located in the Gràcia neighbourhood of Barcelona, it offers degrees in performance (all instrument families), composition, musicology, music education, and jazz. Admission is by audition and theoretical exam. Very competitive, but publicly funded with scholarship programmes and university-level fees. If you are aiming for a professional or academic career at the highest level, this is where the path starts.

The Conservatori Superior Municipal de Música de Barcelona, located near Paral·lel, is the second higher education option in Barcelona. Classical and contemporary specialisations, with a long tradition in the city. Less internationally known than ESMUC but with an excellent faculty.

The Conservatori Professional Municipal de Música de Barcelona offers professional-level studies (equivalent to secondary education). Located in the Eixample district, it is the natural route for young musicians wanting the official qualification without leaving Barcelona. Admission from age 12, by instrument audition.

The Escola Municipal de Música de Barcelona covers elementary and introductory education, with several locations across the city’s neighbourhoods. A good entry point for beginners, or for adults who want to learn without the pressure of a professional conservatory programme.

Outside Barcelona, every provincial capital and major city has its own professional conservatory: Sabadell, Terrassa, Girona, Tarragona, Lleida. If you live outside the metropolitan area, the conservatory in your nearest city is the starting reference for official music education.

Jazz, modern, and popular music

Taller de Músics is the reference school for jazz, blues, funk, soul, and modern music in Catalonia. It combines a school with a concert venue (the Jamboree, on La Rambla), which creates a unique ecosystem: you study with teachers who perform professionally, and you have direct access to live music programming. It offers both accredited and non-accredited courses, with day and evening schedules. Very active in adult and working-musician education. Some courses available in English.

The Escola de Música Moderna i de Jazz (EMMJ) in the Poblenou neighbourhood is another solid private option, specialising in modern music, music production, and contemporary vocal technique.

Can Ponsic — Escola Municipal de Música in Sant Gervasi-Galvany is a municipal school with a well-structured modern music programme, particularly strong for young students.

For international certification in popular music, several schools in Barcelona work with ABRSM (Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music) or Rockschool grading systems — internationally recognised frameworks that allow you to certify your level independently of the Spanish official education system. These are widely understood outside Spain.

Traditional and folk music

Catalan traditional music has its own transmission spaces that do not always overlap with the conservatory system. Instruments of the cobla ensemble (tenora, tible, fiscorn, cobla double bass, flabiol and tamborí), the gralla, the sac de gemecs (bagpipes), and the tabalet are primarily transmitted through specialist groups and schools.

The Casals de Cultura Popular (popular culture centres) and sardanista groups have for decades been the main vehicle for transmitting cobla music and traditional Catalan dance. This is often not formal schooling but learning by immersion and joint rehearsal. For those wanting to learn cobla instruments, the practical path usually involves contacting local groups directly.

For gralla, sac de gemecs, and Mediterranean wind instruments, there are specialist schools and summer courses linked to the Catalan popular culture movement. The Centre de Promoció de la Cultura Popular i Tradicional Catalana (Generalitat de Catalunya) can point you towards schools and groups by area.

Early music and period performance

ESMUC has an active early music department with teachers specialising in viola da gamba, harpsichord, lute, recorder, and historicist performance practice. For those wanting to specialise in period performance, it is one of the few centres in Spain offering higher education in this field.

For complementary training or access to world-class faculty, summer courses are an important option. The Pablo Casals Festival courses at El Vendrell and the early music courses at the Peralada Festival are two of the most notable in the territory. The Escuela Superior de Música Reina Sofía in Madrid (accessible from Catalonia) has specialised early music programmes with European faculty of international standing.

Flamenco

Barcelona has a genuine flamenco scene, not a tourist one. Specialist schools in the Raval and Gràcia neighbourhoods offer training in flamenco technique — dance, guitar, and cante. The peñas flamencas — cultural associations rooted in the Andalusian community and flamenco enthusiasts — remain an informal but tradition-rich learning space with direct contact with the art form. For those wanting to learn flamenco seriously, combining technical school training with participation in peñas is the route many performers take.

How to choose the right school

Three concrete questions to guide you:

What is your current level? If you are starting from scratch or are at elementary level, municipal schools and private elementary centres are the entry point. If you already play and want the official qualification, the professional conservatory is the step. If you have the professional degree and want an academic or professional career, the higher conservatory is the path.

Do you need an official qualification, or is free training enough? If you want to work professionally in academic music, education, or public orchestras, the official qualification matters. If you want to perform professionally in jazz, pop, folk, or modern music circuits, free training (Taller de Músics, private schools, online platforms) often opens doors faster and with less time investment.

How much time can you commit? Official professional and higher conservatories require full-time or near-full-time dedication, which is incompatible with a full-time job. Modern music schools and private centres typically run evening and weekend schedules built for working adults. This is a major practical difference that often goes unmentioned.

The answers to these three questions will point you to the right type of school without needing to visit ten different centres.
